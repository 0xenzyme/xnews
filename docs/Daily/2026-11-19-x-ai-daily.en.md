### Google launches Gemini 4 Neon: between Carbon and Hydrogen, built to finish long work fast

- What happened: @sundarpichai, @GoogleDeepMind, @OfficialLoganK, and @demishassabis announced Gemini 4 Neon in sync. The official blog (Koray Kavukcuoglu, Nov 18) places it between Carbon and Hydrogen for complex software engineering, knowledge work, and science workflows, with an emphasis on finishing long, multi-step jobs. Official figures: #3 overall on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at xhigh with a score of 61 (ahead of comparable models, just behind larger, slower ones); #2 on the Vals Index (70.9%), and #1 on the Legal Research Bench and Tax Agent Bench; #2 on the Agent Arena Index (1336, across 4,200+ real agent sessions) and #2 on steerability. On science it claims best-in-class Terminal-Bench Science 70.5%, RiemannBench 84.0%, and HLE-Diamond 71.3%. On coding, #1 on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index at 58.6, 65.2% on Terminal-Bench 4.1, and 46.2% on FrontierCode 2.0 at the default high setting. API pricing is $3 / $0.15 / $15 per million input / cached input / output tokens (a 95% cache discount, per Google) with a 1M-token context. At default high, Google says cost per task is 32% below Carbon; at high on Vals and Agent Arena it sits on the Pareto frontier, with roughly 50%–80% lower cost per task than top competing frontier models. Four thinking levels are available. Starting today: Google AI Pro / Ultra users get it in the Gemini app, Google AI Studio, and paid Antigravity; developers and enterprises get the paid API in AI Studio and Android Studio, plus Gemini for business. Safety is described as Carbon-level against severe misuse and prompt injection; “Neon for cyber” will roll out in the coming days to approved defenders in the Fairwind Program.

### Tencent Hunyuan open-weights Hy4 flash: ~220B total, ~12B active, plus FP8 and Base

- What happened: @TencentHunyuan released open weights for Hy4 flash, alongside an FP8 instruct build and the pretrained Hy4 flash-Base, on Hugging Face, ModelScope, and GitCode; the GitHub repo is [Tencent-Hunyuan/Hy4-flash](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hy4-flash). The model card: about 220B total parameters (including ~64B Engram), about 12B active per token (11.3B text + 0.43B vision), native text/image in and text out, 1M-token context, under the Tencent Hy License. Architecture notes include hybrid attention (IndexPool sparse attention interleaved 1:3 with sliding-window attention), LatentMoE, and a built-in 0.85B DFly2 drafter for speculative decoding. The official X post rounded the size to “224B (12B active)”; this issue follows the model card. The model itself launched as an API on Nov 16; this is the open-weight drop two days later. @opencode called it “an unexpected hit” and said it remains exclusively free on OpenCode for the next 10 days.
- Why it matters: Among recent everyday-productivity models from Chinese labs, this is a fast follow-through from API to weights — you can run a side-by-side on high-end single-GPU or multi-GPU boxes, not just on a price sheet.

## 4. Chips and infrastructure

### OpenAI: models found up to 14% PPA gains on AMD flagship GPU IP; some edits have gone to the fab

- What happened: @OpenAI posted and published “Advancing chip design with OpenAI models.” It says that, working with AMD on flagship GPU IP that engineers had already spent years optimizing, the models rewrote SystemVerilog RTL, checked behavior with Synopsys tools, and found up to 14% PPA improvement — “roughly a year of progress in less than five months.” AMD has already included some of those changes in a design sent for manufacturing and added others to production files for later versions. AMD also verified single-digit PPA gains on key blocks of its next flagship CPU, using designs written by the model. On OpenAI’s own first custom inference chip, Jalapeño, model-proposed edits were silicon-proven, with estimated standard-cell area cuts of about 13% in the vector unit and 10% in the matrix unit. Next, OpenAI is training GPT-Synopsys with Synopsys so models can use EDA tools the way chip designers do.
- Why it matters: This moves “models edit code” into “models edit RTL that is actually going to a fab,” and the customer is AMD’s production line, not just an open teaching core.
- Source & engagement: [OpenAI](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2123100386638877054)｜~528k impressions, 3,785 likes, 614 bookmarks; canonical post: [OpenAI blog](https://openai.com/index/advancing-chip-design-with-our-models/)
- Verification: The blog and official post are verified. The 14% figure, silicon-proven area cuts, “a year of progress,” and the CPU single-digit gains are OpenAI official author-claim; we did not see a first-party AMD note naming the part and the blocks this issue.
- Take: Treat this as evidence that AI design assist is inside production RTL, not as a promise that a shipping GPU is already 14% faster. The checkable moment is whether AMD’s next flagship launch or earnings commentary attributes wins to these edits.
