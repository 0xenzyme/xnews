## 1. The harness debate

### Garry Tan's harness evals + the Harness Leaderboard

- What happened: YC president Garry Tan shared early results from his open-source harness evals (project name GHarnessEvals) on Nov 8. From his screenshot: 7 setups (Claude Code, OpenClaw, Hermes and QM on Opus; Codex, OpenClaw and Hermes on Sol) ran the same 20 coding tasks and all passed 71–79%. 14 of the 20 tasks scored identically everywhere, which he calls a test problem rather than a tie; a harder Terminal-Bench rerun is in progress. Cost differs a lot: on Opus, OpenClaw pays about $0.44 per solved task vs $0.22 for Claude Code, $0.24 for Hermes and $0.19 for QM; OpenClaw takes ~99 s per try vs ~40 s for Claude Code; on Sol, Codex is cheapest and fastest ($0.17). For contrast, Joël Niklaus released the [Harness Leaderboard](https://huggingface.co/spaces/joelniklaus/harness-leaderboard) on Nov 5: 28,849 trials ($4,746) on GLM-5.3-Flash, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash and Flint with model and effort fixed. Bash-only mini-swe-agent averages 51.4% vs OpenClaw 27.3%; Pi hits 49.4% at $0.29 per solved task (~45% below mini-swe-agent's $0.53); OpenCode costs $3.35; the best harness changes with the model.
- Why it matters: On frontier closed models the wrapper gap gets washed out by model strength; on open models it can be 24 points. Rerun the harness choice on your own model instead of copying someone's leaderboard.
- Source & engagement: [garrytan](https://x.com/garrytan/status/2119492448200405110), ~55K views, 349 likes, 91 replies, 144 bookmarks; [joelniklaus](https://x.com/joelniklaus/status/2118373808692437403), ~315K views, 2,569 likes, 2,754 bookmarks.
- Verification: author-claim for both (individual evals, methods public, no third-party replication). Garry's numbers come from his screenshot; the Terminal-Bench rerun has no results yet.
- Take: Fine to say "how much the harness matters depends on the model"; don't write "harnesses don't matter" or crown a winner.

## 2. Chinese agent platform

## 3. Open-model values are one fine-tune away (catch-up)

### Nous Research releases Arete-27B

- What happened: @NousResearch released [Arete-27B](https://nousresearch.com/arete) on Nov 6: Qwen3.8-27B with ~52.8M tokens of supervised fine-tuning (no RL), using Inkling as teacher. On an in-house 803-prompt eval across 7 value categories, answers adopting the official party line fell from 22% to ~1% and the fact score rose from 2.32 to 2.98 (of 5); Hermes Bench is 65.60% vs 65.77% for the base. Weights, data and eval are public. The blog lists GLM 5.3 at 15% and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash at 16% on the same metric.
- Why it matters: Chinese models are now the dominant open base in the West ([Menlo Ventures, Nov 3](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-enterprise-ai/): 89% of US enterprise open-model spend, Kimi alone 61%). This turns "Chinese model values" into something you can measure and modify. Reactions split sharply.
- Source & engagement: [NousResearch](https://x.com/NousResearch/status/2118822925445050435), ~2.23M views, 14,539 likes, 1,457 reposts, 1,207 quotes, 7,669 bookmarks.
- Verification: verified (official blog plus public weights and data); the eval is Nous-built and LLM-judged, so treat scores as author-claim. Critics say it swaps one bias for another.
- Take: Attribute "the bias layer is shallow" to Nous; don't draw political conclusions for either side.

## 4. AI does math

### PSI: AI system proves the Aaronson–Ambainis conjecture, checked in Lean (catch-up)

- What happened: Physical Superintelligence (PSI) announced on Nov 6 that its internal research system, using a non-public Anthropic model, found a complete proof of the 2008 Aaronson–Ambainis conjecture on day three of a run. Lean accepted it, and the system later improved the bound twice. The [paper and Lean 4 formalization](https://github.com/physical-superintelligence/aaronson-ambainis) are public; the repo reports no placeholders or added axioms and re-checks by two independent Lean kernel implementations. Implication: superpolynomial quantum speedups in the query model require structure in the inputs.
- Why it matters: After last week's DeepMind RSA result, this is another AI plus formal verification win on an open problem, and the materials are fully checkable.
- Source & engagement: [psipbc](https://x.com/psipbc/status/2118706269506949152), ~1.11M views, 3,983 likes, 663 reposts, 2,223 bookmarks.
- Verification: verified (official announcement plus public code). Lean checks the formal statement; whether it exactly matches the printed conjecture still needs expert review, a trust boundary commentators flagged.
- Take: "Lean-verified AI proof" is fair; "quantum computing is useless" is not, since the result covers the query model on unstructured inputs.

## 6. AI accelerating AI R&D (catch-up)

### METR: Anthropic is improving about 2x faster, but most evidence is redacted

- What happened: METR published its [independent assessment](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-05-anthropic-ai-rd-acceleration-assessment/) on Nov 5 after on-site access from Aug 12 to Oct 29, including internal data and unreleased models. Since October 2025, Anthropic's benchmark scores have grown about 1.9x faster than before (95% CI 1.5–2.8x), not explained by faster compute scaling, and most of the acceleration is likely from Anthropic's heavy use of AI in R&D. METR also says Anthropic redacted a majority of the evidence it found most informative. The debate resurfaced on X this weekend, mostly about findings that seem credible but can't be checked.
- Why it matters: The first outside quantitative estimate of AI-driven acceleration at a frontier lab.
- Source & engagement: [METR_Evals](https://x.com/METR_Evals/status/2118412183860351084), ~1.78M views, 5,279 likes, 684 reposts, 3,402 bookmarks.

- What happened: BLA (Blind LLM Agents), built by Chinese developer Timo Zhang (@xteam3301, GitHub qybaihe), recirculated in Chinese, English and French this weekend.
