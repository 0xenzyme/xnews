## 1. Model launches

### OpenAI ships GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol: a cheaper frontier model plus a daily driver for agents

- What happened: @OpenAI launched GPT-6.2 Astra, a frontier model for the hardest projects, and GPT-6.2 Sol, an efficient model for everyday work and multistep agents. The official blog says Astra sets a new high on Agents’ Last Exam (long-horizon professional work), 5.2 points above GPT-6 Astra at less than one-third the cost per task, and 5.5 points ahead of Claude Fable 5.2 at about 5% of the cost (footnote: Fable 5.2’s leaderboard score excludes nine tasks that used a fallback model). On Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1 it is 9 points above GPT-6 Astra, again at less than one-third the cost; OpenAI calls it the best model in the world for scientists. API prices: Astra is $8 / $0.40 / $40 per million tokens for input / cached input / output — 20% below GPT-6 Astra on input and output, with a 60% discount on cached input. Sol keeps GPT-6.1 Sol pricing at $2 / $0.10 / $10. From today, Plus, Pro, Enterprise, and Edu users can use both models in ChatGPT Work and Codex; the API names are `gpt-6.2-astra` and `gpt-6.2-sol`, with Ultrafast on both. Pro 500 gets Ultrafast on either model; Pro 200 gets Ultrafast for the first time, starting with Sol. OpenRouter listed both, including Pro variants. On safety, both models meet the Preparedness Framework’s Critical bar for cybersecurity and High for biological and chemical capabilities. Astra is also the first generally released OpenAI model to meet High for AI Self-improvement. Because capabilities went up, higher-risk cyber assistance is more tightly refused; OpenAI says it will bring the 6.2 models to Daybreak so approved defenders can see fewer refusals. An official post also says Astra is up 22 points on ExploitBench Internal Port versus GPT-6 Astra.
- Why it matters: This is a direct answer to the “Fable / Opus owns coding and professional work” narrative, fought on cost per task and professional artifacts rather than a single leaderboard. The Self-improvement High designation is OpenAI admitting the model can speed up its own research.
- Source & engagement: [OpenAI main post](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2122750124543926515) — about 4.73M impressions and 29k likes; [Astra positioning](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2122750126297161811) — about 650k impressions; [Sol positioning](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2122750128176222464); [cyber and safeguards](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2122750133133791592); [availability](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2122750135608528984); [OpenRouter](https://x.com/OpenRouter/status/2122754139533066653); official [blog](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-2-astra-sol) and [system card](https://deploymentsafety.openai.com/gpt-6-2-astra-sol/safety-overview)

### Reuters exclusive: China is distilling US model outputs, now as an industrial practice

- What happened: Reuters (Eduardo Baptista and Laurie Chen, Beijing, November 17) reports that Chinese organizations are using increasingly systematic methods to turn outputs from leading US models into training data for domestic systems, as Washington tightens advanced-chip export rules. Reuters says it reviewed more than 300 papers, patents, corporate disclosures, job ads, and government documents, and that distillation has moved from academic experiments into mainstream industrial use; Beijing, it says, is also encouraging distillation to make AI cheaper to deploy in manufacturing. The story stresses that distillation is not illegal and cannot copy a frontier model wholesale, but it may blunt the effect of chip controls. It also says US Commerce officials raised IP concerns in Chengdu talks; Chinese officials called distillation a standard technique and said they would act if it amounted to theft, without concrete commitments.
- Why it matters: This goes to whether Chinese teams can keep using closed-API traces as synthetic data, and how contracts and export-control enforcement may tighten next.
- Source & engagement: [Reuters](https://x.com/Reuters/status/2122537762439975368) — about 188k impressions and 141 likes; the story is the Reuters exclusive (reprints include [Yahoo](https://ca.finance.yahoo.com/news/exclusive-china-taps-us-ai-034212581.html))
- Verification: Publication of the exclusive is verified. The Chengdu meeting details come from unnamed sources (author-claim). We could not independently measure how widely each company uses the practice.
- Take: If your data pipeline depends on closed US APIs for synthetic traces, reread the provider terms and keep a backup path that does not.
