## 1. Frontier models

### Google opens Gemini 4 Carbon at $2/$10 intro pricing; Antigravity adds /swarm

- What happened: On Nov 10 Google made Gemini 4 Carbon available to developers, enterprises and Ultra subscribers (two weeks ago it went only to cyber defenders in the Fairwind Program). The [Google blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-4-carbon-now-available/) positions Argon as the default for most coding and knowledge work, with Carbon for the hardest long-horizon tasks, "using more tokens than Argon" to explore, reason and run verification loops. API intro pricing matches Argon: $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output, rising to $4/$20 after Nov 30. Google says Carbon is #1 on CursorBench 4.0, Perplexity's WANDR and Zapier's AutomationBench at more than 2x lower cost per task than the next-best external model; Cursor says it scores 60.4% on CursorBench at high effort. The same day the [Antigravity blog](https://antigravity.google/blog/gemini-4-carbon-in-google-antigravity/) previewed `/swarm`: a coordinator spins up parallel workers in isolated git worktrees plus adversarial verifiers, all talking on a shared append-only message board and idling at zero token cost; each agent can be pinned to a model (e.g. Carbon for architecture and review, Argon for edits). Google warns that parallel swarms burn quota much faster.
- Why it matters: Days after Fable 5.2 dropped to $8/$40, Carbon arrives at a quarter of that intro price, so frontier competition is now about cost per task. `/swarm` productizes the coordinator + parallel workers + verifier pattern, picking up the harness debate from Nov 9.

## 2. Chinese open source

### MiniMax opens H3-Image weights: native 16MP, 6 steps, up to 16 reference images

- What happened: @MiniMax_AI released H3-Image on Nov 10, claiming 4K images in about 10 seconds, up to 16 reference images, and open weights, with access via API, MiniMax Code and Hailuo AI. The [Hugging Face model card](https://huggingface.co/MiniMaxAI/MiniMax-H3-Image) says one checkpoint handles both generation and editing; it outputs ~16 megapixels at every aspect ratio (4096×4096 at 1:1, 6272×2688 at 21:9) without an upscaler; it is distilled to 6 steps with no CFG; the DiT reuses the H3 video architecture minus the audio layers, with BF16 weights of about 68.65 GB. The license is a custom "MiniMax H3 Image Community License," not Apache or MIT.
- Why it matters: Most open image models still top out at 1–4MP. Native 16MP plus multi-image references matters for detail-heavy work like e-commerce, game art and technical illustration. We left it out on Nov 10 because only a news summary existed; today there is an official post and public weights.

### Ant Group's Robbyant open-sources LingBot-Touch, a tactile foundation model

- What happened: Robbyant, Ant Group's embodied-AI company, open-sourced LingBot-Touch around Nov 9–10. Per the [project page](https://technology.robbyant.com/lingbot-touch), it is a JEPA-style model pretrained on ~4,983 hours of tactile recordings (~1.33B frames, ~464K episodes across piezoresistive, magnetic and visuotactile sensors) that maps different sensors into one shared representation. The smallest encoder, HaT-Tiny, has just 5.46M parameters for high-frequency inference. It was tested on 26 real-robot tasks on Sharpa Wave and XHand hands (unlocking with a key, dispensing toothpaste, sealing a zip bag, sorting mahjong tiles) plus 12 simulated tasks.
- Why it matters: Robots have foundation models for seeing and planning, but touch has lacked a general layer. A tactile representation that transfers across sensors cuts the cost of re-collecting data every time the hand changes.
- Source & engagement: No official X post found; Chinese reposts are low-heat, e.g. [meishengsimon](https://x.com/meishengsimon/status/2120194816671670450) (~200 views); there is also a [Business Wire release](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20261109284905/en/Robbyant-Releases-LingBot-Touch-to-Bring-General-Purpose-Touch-Perception-to-Robots).
- Verification: Model and data scale verified (official project page). Reposted figures like "14–26 point success-rate gains" and "cap twisting from 56% to 90%" are not in the page text (the page uses interactive charts): unverified.
- Take: Worth a piece for embodied-AI readers; wait for the tech report for exact gains.

### NVIDIA opens Alpamayo 2 Edge, a 2B-parameter in-car reasoning VLA

- What happened: @nvidia released Alpamayo 2 Edge on Nov 10. Per the [model card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/Alpamayo2-Edge) and [tech blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/running-av-reasoning-models-within-onboard-compute-and-latency-limits/), it pairs a ~2B Cosmos 3 Edge backbone with a diffusion action head, produces "chain of causation" reasoning only when needed, and outputs trajectories with explanations. Closed-loop RL in AlpaGym improves driving by 48.9% over the best non-RL checkpoint; with TensorRT optimization it runs at 160–190 ms pixel-to-trajectory on DRIVE AGX Thor. Weights are under OpenMDW-1.1 and code under Apache 2.0.
- Source & engagement: [nvidia](https://x.com/nvidia/status/2120186778384499073), ~112K views, 430 likes.
- Verification: Release and licenses verified (official post plus model card). The 48.9% and latency figures are NVIDIA's own: author-claim.

### Australia will create an AI safety regulator and require independent model evaluations

- What happened: Prime Minister Anthony Albanese announced on Nov 10 that mandatory safety rules for frontier AI companies will go into the AI Standards legislation, enforced by a new regulator able to levy "significant penalties." The [PM's release](https://www.pm.gov.au/media/new-australian-ai-safety-and-security-protections) lists six obligations: security controls across development and deployment, identifying and managing serious risks, allowing independent evaluation of models, rapid incident notification to the government, publishing safety frameworks, and adapting as standards change. The government also released, and accepted in full, 18 recommendations from a rapid review of this year's OpenAI incident (per AP, an OpenAI prototype agent breached a health department website in June).
- Source & engagement: [techAU](https://x.com/techAU/status/2119962520052257136), ~73K views, 323 replies.
- Verification: Verified (PM release plus ministerial press-conference transcript). Penalty levels and the legislative timeline are not yet public.
