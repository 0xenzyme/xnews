## 1. Math and AI

### Alpöge and Claude's Riemann Hypothesis proof: the Lean and Mathlib communities say they have "full confidence"

- What happened: On Nov 20, Alpöge posted a one-page [remark.pdf](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf) saying that, "working with Claude," he discovered a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis, that the method extends to the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, and that "Claude and I have also formalized the arguments without assumptions." The Lean code ships as 14 archive parts. He writes that after he steered them to a setting analogous to a toy model, Claude supplied several decisive steps near the finish, such as a chain of theta lifts of length √X that mimics multiplicity in Stepanov's method. On Nov 21 the [Lean community blog](https://leanprover-community.github.io/blog/posts/riemann-hypothesis/) published a joint statement endorsed by the Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative. The library is about 17 million lines and depends directly only on Mathlib; dozens of people reproduced the build and the same raw proof export; the community isolated the statement in a Mathlib-only file and used two tools (comparator and con-leche) to confirm that the proved theorem is exactly Mathlib's RiemannHypothesis; and the export passed Lean's kernel plus several independently implemented checkers. Scientific American quotes MIT mathematician Andrew Sutherland: the proof was rechecked by the Lean kernel using only the three standard axioms, and "the chance that anything is fishy here is approximately zero."
- Why it matters: This is the first time a Millennium-Prize-level conjecture has received a "statement and proof both correct" verdict from the formalization community within about a day of release, with the author openly crediting key steps to a commercial model. It is a new reference point for anyone building math agents or formal tools, or arguing about whether AI can do original research.
- Source & engagement: [Lean FRO post (joint statement)](https://x.com/leanprover/status/2124159121645801562) — about 1.98M impressions, 22,541 likes, 3,641 bookmarks; the post that spread it widest was [@yifanzhang_](https://x.com/yifanzhang_/status/2123789252756414577) — about 14.92M impressions, 60,707 likes, 20,221 bookmarks; [Timothy Gowers](https://x.com/wtgowers/status/2123834355692769586) — about 73K impressions, 1,291 likes. Primary: [remark.pdf](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf), [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-ai-riemann-hypothesis-proof-is-almost-certainly-true-independent-mathematicians-attest/).
- Verification: The correctness of the formal proof is verified, per the Lean/Mathlib joint statement and the independent rechecks Scientific American cites. Which steps Claude contributed, and how much, is Alpöge's own account: author-claim. No full human-readable paper is out yet; he calls the current argument "incredibly long" and plans to write it properly.
- Take: Write "the Riemann Hypothesis now has a formally verified proof." Don't write "Claude proved the Riemann Hypothesis on its own." The division of labor rests on one person's account.

### Same day: Alpöge and Buckmaster claim Navier–Stokes blow-up; the key numerical certificate is not public

- What happened: Gowers' post also linked an "extra present": the GitHub repo [tristanbuckmaster/navier-stokes](https://github.com/tristanbuckmaster/navier-stokes/), created Nov 20, holding a 79-page paper by L. Alpöge and T. Buckmaster, "Finite-time blow-up for the Navier–Stokes equations," and a Lean 4 formalization. Groundy's walkthrough notes that the numerical certificate and the 128-bit interval-arithmetic check are missing from the public repo, and the authors say the patched Lean package was not rebuilt when the repo was assembled. The Next Web relays Terence Tao's view: the paper is technically human-readable and its strategy is clear (build a numerical approximate self-similar profile, then use rigorous perturbation theory to get an exact blow-up solution), but a properly written proof needs more work.
- Why it matters: If it holds, it settles another Millennium problem in the negative (smooth solutions can blow up). Right now the public artifacts let you check the formal layer, not the whole argument.
- Source & engagement: [Gowers](https://x.com/wtgowers/status/2123834355692769586) — about 73K impressions, 1,291 likes; primary: [paper PDF](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/tristanbuckmaster/navier-stokes/main/paper/finite-time-blow-up-navier-stokes.pdf), [Groundy](https://groundy.com/articles/verifying-the-navier-stokes-blow-up-claim-what-the-lean-4-formalization-covers/), [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/navier-stokes-blow-up-without-force-buckmaster-alpoge).
- Verification: The paper and repo exist: verified. The result itself is unverified; no independent mathematician has publicly confirmed it, and the certificate is not out. Tao's remarks are media-relayed and we did not find the original: author-claim.
- Take: "Claimed, pending verification." Don't let the Riemann verdict carry this one.

## 2. Models and open source

### Thinking Machines ships Inkling 2 Mini: a 26.4B looped transformer, ~20GB, laptop-class (catch-up)

- What happened: On Nov 20 (early Nov 21 Shanghai), Thinking Machines Lab released [Inkling 2 Mini](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/), a MoE with 26.4B total and 3.4B active parameters that runs its middle layers three times per token, or 5.4B weight activations per token counting the loops. Most weights are trained and served in NVFP4, with the last four layers in MXFP8, for a ~20GB footprint. The lab says it leads open-weights models under 100B on DeepSWE v1.1 (48.2%) and Vals Index v2.2 (46.5%); the launch post cites 20.1 on the AA Index. It also released a variable-loop research model: no-CoT time horizon rose from 16 seconds at one pass to 37 seconds at three, chain of thought got shorter but stayed legible, and agentic-sabotage scores did not rise with more loops.
- Why it matters: Looping lets a model think more and write less, which is a monitorability question as much as a performance one. Mira Murati wrote that at this scale its thought process remains monitorable, and "this may not stay the case."
- Source & engagement: [@thinkymachines](https://x.com/thinkymachines/status/2123871643797995872) — about 3.04M impressions, 17,442 likes, 14,449 bookmarks; [Mira Murati](https://x.com/miramurati/status/2123872534970208421) — about 693K impressions, 8,667 likes. Community test: [@bebuu_ai vs Qwen 3.8 27B on a 32GB M5 Pro](https://x.com/bebuu_ai/status/2124067425113329707) — 310 likes.
- Verification: Release, sizes, and architecture verified (official page). Benchmarks are self-reported: author-claim; the page notes eval setups vary by model. Community tests are anecdotal: unverified.
- Take: A small open model worth running locally; the story is the looped architecture, not another leaderboard number.

## 3. Developer infrastructure and data security

### OpenRouter warns MiniMax-bound requests may have been read by a third party; three hosts admit forwarding

- What happened: On Nov 21, OpenRouter emailed customers under the subject "Requests you sent to MiniMax models may have been read." Per Privacy Affairs: a security researcher planted unique test links in prompts sent to MiniMax models between July and October; the links were opened 40 to 130 hours later, whether MiniMax served the request or a host forwarded it; the same tests on other models at those hosts were untouched, with one exception; and planted AWS test keys were not used. OpenRouter names AtlasCloud, Novita, and StreamLake as hosts that told it they forwarded MiniMax requests to MiniMax, and it removed the Zero Data Retention (ZDR) labels from those endpoints. Novita then posted a [privacy notice](https://novita.ai/blog/minimax-requests-privacy-notice/): a routing configuration meant for specific customers applied more broadly; MiniMax requests through Novita (including those routed via OpenRouter) between 2026-05-28 and 2026-11-11 may have been accessed or retained by a third party; access patterns "suggest automated content review may have been involved"; affected models are suspended, and users should rotate any secrets they put in prompts.
- Why it matters: ZDR labels and "US-hosted" promises may not hold below the aggregator layer. Anyone calling open models via OpenRouter or third-party hosts should confirm who actually processes the request.
- Source & engagement: [@michael_chomsky's summary](https://x.com/michael_chomsky/status/2124008002571428224) — about 304K impressions, 2,009 likes, 946 bookmarks; [@nateberkopec on receiving the email](https://x.com/nateberkopec/status/2123992018850595164) — about 64K impressions, 799 likes; [Novita's reply](https://x.com/novita_labs/status/2124196616358010992). Report: [Privacy Affairs](https://www.privacyaffairs.com/openrouter-minimax-prompts/).
- Verification: Novita's confirmation verified (official notice). OpenRouter's email is known through recipients and press; it was not on OpenRouter's announcements page: author-claim. MiniMax's explanation (content moderation on before Oct 22, ZDR enabled since) is relayed via StreamLake, with no public MiniMax statement: unverified.
- Take: Rotate any keys that ever sat in a prompt. Longer term, pin the processor in your contract or self-host.

## 4. Policy and chips

### House committees urge removing Chinese open-weight models from federal systems; Cursor says it will explore phasing them out

- What happened: On Nov 20 the House Homeland Security Committee and the Select Committee on the CCP released a [joint report](https://homeland.house.gov/2026/11/20/chairmen-garbarino-moolenaar-release-joint-investigative-report-urging-removal-of-prc-open-weight-models-from-federal-systems-high-risk-critical-infrastructure/). Treasury Secretary Bessent said the same day that AI attacks "are all coming from Chinese models."
- Why it matters: Heavy US use of Chinese open models is turning from a technical choice into a compliance question, a procurement signal for overseas users of Qwen, Kimi, GLM, and DeepSeek.
