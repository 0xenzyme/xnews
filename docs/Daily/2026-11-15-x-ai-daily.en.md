## 1. Security

### Cursor publishes its October incident post-mortem: a Fireworks-exposed key, and up to 5,000 users who may have installed a malicious Claude Code extension

- What happened: Cursor's blog (Nov 13) says an attacker obtained an access key Cursor had issued to Fireworks, its inference/training vendor (the key was exposed on Fireworks' side), and copied coding-agent traces from a shared read-only bucket (mostly 2025, from sessions not in Privacy Mode). A few traces held unredacted credentials (under 0.01% of customers had API credentials exposed), which the attacker used to reach some of Cursor's SaaS tools, archived non-production repos, some support email and usage data. On Oct 29 the attacker used a stolen credential to place malicious copies of Anthropic.claude-code 2.1.312 / 2.1.313 on the cache behind Cursor's extension marketplace. They were live for about 11 hours, and up to ~5,000 users may have received them. Cursor says the last attacker activity was Nov 1 and was blocked. It has moved all inference off Fireworks, rotated every internal credential, and rebuilt the marketplace.
- Why it matters: A textbook chain: vendor key → plaintext secrets inside training traces → supply-chain poisoning. Any team using AI coding tools may have secrets sitting in its own agent traces.
- Source & engagement: [cursor_ai](https://x.com/cursor_ai/status/2121411147714970092) · ~557K views, 1,995 likes; post-mortem [cursor.com/blog/security-incident-update](https://cursor.com/blog/security-incident-update); Oct 30 [security notice and check scripts](https://cursor.com/blog/claude-code-extension-security-notice)
- Verification: verified (official blog). Affected counts and "contained" are Cursor's own statements, author-claim. Separate reports tying an Uber driver-data breach to Fireworks credentials were not checked, unverified.
- Take: If you installed or updated the Claude Code extension inside Cursor on Oct 29, run the scripts in the notice. Uninstalling alone is not enough. Teams without Privacy Mode should revisit that setting.

## 5. Research & evals

### Deepnote's Agent Initiative Benchmark; a 200+ model study says keep vision parameters, skip encoder pretraining; 28 models play Mafia

- What happened: Deepnote's @jakubjurovych introduced a proactive-agent benchmark: 12 frontier models listen in on the Slack of a synthetic company with six years of detailed history and must step in when useful (aligning teams, flagging an unapproved customer logo). OpenAI's 6.1 Sol and Astra beat Anthropic's Fable 5.2. Ellis Brown and colleagues (Meta FAIR / NYU) trained 200+ multimodal models from scratch: dedicated vision parameters matter, while standalone encoders and separate pretraining are optional; encoder-free designs still lag. A Mafia-style game with 250K humans and 28 models claims OpenAI models lie most, xAI's are most loyal, Claude snitches most, and a fruit-fly brain model also played.
- Why it matters: Evals are shifting from single-answer accuracy to deployment questions: when to act, how to wire vision, how models behave socially.
