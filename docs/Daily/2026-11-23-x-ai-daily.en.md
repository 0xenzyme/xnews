## 1. Safety and research

### OpenAI publishes internal pretraining perplexity; Pachocki proposes "common limits" for frontier models

- What happened: On Nov 22 OpenAI published Pachocki's essay [Perplexity and Intelligence](https://openai.com/index/perplexity-and-intelligence). It reports log-perplexity (cross-entropy loss) on a held-out set drawn from OpenAI's internal codebase: GPT-3.5 at ~0.99, GPT-4 at ~0.91, and recent models below 0.8, where he says "transformative capabilities" emerged in cybersecurity and mathematics. After upgrading to a model at ~0.76, it began resolving long-standing open math problems "one after another in a single Slack thread." He expects new capabilities, possibly ones nobody tests for yet, in the 0.68–0.7 window, alongside further degradation of current alignment auditing, and says an "avalanche" of pretraining research advances could come as soon as 2027. He proposes that frontier developers agree on a first limit based on perplexity and other measurements, not to be crossed until monitoring reaches a concrete milestone such as a successor to CoT monitors. He also lists four weaknesses: cross-lab datasets differ, the metric maps to no specific task, it misses gains outside pretraining such as chain-of-thought, and its floor is unknown.
- Why it matters: A frontier lab publishing its own pretraining progress and proposing a cross-lab "pause line" is rare. Sam Altman called it "the most important thing OpenAI has published in awhile." A footnote says the 2026 curve accelerates sharply, mostly from long-term research and compute, with "meaningful design contributions coming from AI" in the latest models.

- Verification: essay and figures verified (OpenAI's page). "Transformative capabilities" and the 0.68–0.7 forecast are the author's judgment, author-claim. The numbers are on OpenAI's internal code set and are not comparable to other labs as-is.
- Take: A proposal, not a commitment. Watch whether Anthropic and Google DeepMind publish comparable numbers; without a shared dataset, a "common limit" is hard to enforce.

### Riemann Hypothesis follow-ups: two human-readable reconstructions appear; an "invalid formalization" paper gets no community support

- What happened: Per a [verification log](https://riemannhypothesisclaude.org/verification-log) that tracks the proof: on Nov 21, Michael Rubinstein (Waterloo) posted a 164-page third-party working draft explaining the zeta case, which still takes one key inequality, (M), from the Lean code without proving it by hand; Jimmie Adriazola (Arizona State) posted a 66-page end-to-end reconstruction in conventional mathematics that he says is not yet complete. Separately, Jenny Lorraine Nielsen posted an unreviewed PhilArchive paper arguing the proof is invalid because a geometry predicate on the path is `anyGeometry := fun _ _ _ => True`; she accepts that the final theorem typechecks with only standard axioms. As of this edition neither Alpöge nor the Lean community has responded, and the Lean/Mathlib certification stands. Two noisier threads ran on X: independent researcher Robert Edward Grant says he wrote to Anthropic's CEO about "overlaps" with his geometric research, and a rumor that a polynomial-time factoring algorithm is "close," which its poster said to take "with an entire shaker of salt."
- Why it matters: The point of a formal proof is that if the final statement is Mathlib's RiemannHypothesis and the term passes the kernel with standard axioms, a vacuous intermediate definition does not make the theorem false; at most it means the argument doesn't run the way the prose describes. What is still missing is a proof humans can read.

## 2. Models and open source

### Ant's inclusionAI open-sources LingSante-3.1-flash: 560B / 25B-active medical model, 1M context, MIT

- What happened: On Nov 22 Ant's Ling team released [LingSante-3.1-flash](https://huggingface.co/inclusionAI/LingSante-3.1-flash), built on Ling-3.1-flash: MoE with 560B total and 25B active parameters per token, 1M context, MIT license on the model card, plus an MXFP4 build. The team says it beats GPT-6 Astra, Claude Opus 5.5 and Gemini 4 Argon on HealthBench Professional (65.72), HLE text-only with tools (72.22) and BrowseComp (94.10), and scores 74.40 on EHR-Complex. A multimodal sibling, LingSante-3.1-air-VL (124B total / 5.5B active), covers medical imaging and report generation. The model card says it is for research and evaluation, is not a medical device, and must not be the sole basis for diagnosis, prescriptions or treatment.
- Why it matters: One of the largest open medical-specialist models so far, under a permissive license, so hospitals and health SaaS can self-host. Open weights solve "data stays in-house," not clinical liability.
- Source & engagement: [@AntLingAGI](https://x.com/AntLingAGI/status/2124523916819648945) | ~289K views, 1,661 likes, 768 bookmarks; [air-VL post](https://x.com/AntLingAGI/status/2124555085640610194) | ~175K views, 539 likes.
- Verification: weights, size and MIT license verified (Hugging Face model card). Wins over closed models are self-reported, author-claim.
- Take: Worth evaluating if you build medical agents. ~574GB of FP8 weights is not a single-box toy; the MXFP4 build is the realistic start.
