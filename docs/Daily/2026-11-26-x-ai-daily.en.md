## 1. AI and mathematics

### OpenAI preprint: the matrix multiplication exponent is exactly 9/4

- What happened: OpenAI published the November 24 preprint "The matrix multiplication exponent is nine fourths" in its openai/math repo. The abstract says that over every field, a formal arithmetic circuit multiplying two n×n matrices needs at least n^(9/4)/2 multiplication or division gates. The 9/4 upper bound came with the October 6 batch. This paper adds the matching lower bound, which pins ω at 2.25. Sébastien Bubeck says almost nothing was known about lower bounds before, and the model had been working on one since the upper bound.
- Why it matters: ω has been one of the central open problems in algorithms since Strassen in 1969. If the proof holds, it is the first time AI has independently settled both ends of a textbook complexity question. Engineers working on numerics, compilers or inference kernels see no near-term effect, because nobody knows yet whether the upper-bound construction has usable constants.
- Source & engagement: [@SebastienBubeck](https://x.com/SebastienBubeck/status/2125402576145572334) | ~673K views, 4,303 likes, 867 bookmarks; [@gdb](https://x.com/gdb/status/2125418102049628201) | ~326K views, 4,078 likes; [@deedydas explainer](https://x.com/deedydas/status/2125445769629204816) | ~317K views, 4,784 likes; preprint at [github.com/openai/math](https://github.com/openai/math/tree/main/preprints/The-matrix-multiplication-exponent-is-nine-fourths-November-24-2026).
- Verification: The preprint and abstract are verified (the repo is readable). "The proof is correct" is author-claim. There is no independent check or peer review yet, and this edition did not confirm whether a Lean formalization covers the whole paper.
- Take: Give the complexity-theory community a week to respond. If a result like this has a hole, it is usually in how far the formal-arithmetic-circuit model applies.

## 2. Engineering and infra

### Anthropic moves the Messages API from Python to Rust: Claude wrote v1 in a week, and production checked it

- What happened: Anthropic's engineering blog says traffic to the Messages API grew about 1,800× from early 2024 to fall 2026. This API is the main way customers, Claude Code and Cowork reach Claude, and the growth drove the move to Rust. Claude wrote the core request path in about a week. The hard part was proving parity: the Python service kept changing, model outputs are nondeterministic, and engineers can't read customer requests. Anthropic shadowed production traffic. The same model output ran through both versions, every stage was compared, and differences were recorded as privacy-safe fingerprints. Teams of Claude agents reproduced each difference in a sandbox and proposed a fix, and humans reviewed the fixes. Production match rose from about 24% to 99.6%.
- Why it matters: This is a public case of agents writing a migration and agents verifying it, on a frontier lab's core service. The reusable idea is to treat the old system as the oracle and use difference fingerprints instead of reading raw data.
- Source & engagement: [@AnthropicAI](https://x.com/AnthropicAI/status/2125627317263466739) | ~1.8M views, 7,966 likes, 3,730 bookmarks; blog: [anthropic.com/research/migrating-api-to-rust](https://www.anthropic.com/research/migrating-api-to-rust).
- Verification: The migration and the method are verified (official post plus blog). The ~70% CPU cut per request, ~20% lower median time to first token and ~1,800× traffic growth are author-claim (Anthropic's own figures).
- Take: The must-read of the day for engineering teams. How the fingerprints are designed is worth more of your time than the Rust part.

### OpenAI publishes pretraining perplexity: about 7× faster decline since 2026

- What happened: In "Perplexity and Intelligence," Jakub Pachocki published the log-perplexity of every OpenAI frontier model since GPT-3.5, measured on a fixed code corpus. METR says the decline since early 2026 is about 7× faster than in 2022–2025. Ajeya Cotra says that is all the more striking because the curve should be flattening. Jason Wei notes the dataset wasn't released and shows how to approximate the curve with public data.
- Why it matters: This is the first full pretraining-loss series a frontier lab has published. It is lower-level evidence on acceleration than benchmarks, and it follows Pachocki's recent proposal that labs share perplexity thresholds.
